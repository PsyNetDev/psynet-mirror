A fixed group that plays a loop of cued rounds can now pass the loop value as `round=`, as in `Trial.cue({"question": i}, round=i)`. Each iteration is then the group's round of that number, so a round's definition can come from the loop: a player who joins the group late skips the rounds it finished without them (rounds whose `GroupBarrier` has released the group: a cued round closes only then, so put the barrier inside `show_trial`), and a player who leaves plays the remaining iterations alone. A module whose cues pass `round=` runs once per participant; give each run its own module id. Each member's group membership now stores the cued round they last played or skipped, and a `GroupBarrier` reached in or after a cued round, also between iterations or after the loop, waits only for the members who played that round and haven't passed this barrier in it yet, plus, while the round is still open, the members who haven't reached it and are still in its module. It doesn't wait for members who skipped the round, are in a round the group played after it, are behind a round that has closed, or have left the round's module, as when their loop ended at an earlier round, and it releases the round's skippers with the players, or alone once the players have passed. A barrier that a late joiner reaches before their first cue in the module, such as one at the start of each iteration, waits only for the members who haven't cued a round there either and are still in the module, once the group has; a group that hasn't cued a round in the module waits for every member as before. A release of only such catching-up members, late joiners or skippers, doesn't run `on_release` and doesn't count as the group's barrier pass for `left` or `late_after`. Cueing a round with `round=` again after a later one, even a round the participant skipped, or a trial class again in a round they played, raises an error. So `on_release`'s `participants` and `trials` may now be a subset of the group's active members, and `leader` may not be among them.
