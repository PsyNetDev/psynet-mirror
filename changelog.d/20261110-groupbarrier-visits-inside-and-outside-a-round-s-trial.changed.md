`GroupBarrier` now tells two kinds of visit apart, stored when each member arrives. A barrier inside a cued round's trial (in `show_trial` of a trial class with `sync_group_type`) is a round visit: it waits for the members whose current trial is in that round (not a member who left the group, rejoined and skipped it) and, while the round is open, for those who haven't cued yet; its release closes the round, and `on_release` gets the round's `node`. Any other barrier, such as one before the first cue, in `show_feedback`, at the start of each iteration or of a later run of the module, or after the loop, is a group visit: it waits for every member still in the module, gets `node=None`, and `trials` holds the members' current trials. Neither kind waits for members who have finished the module, `late_after` counts only a visit's own waiters as present, and every release except a member's own time-out is the group's pass, which runs `on_release` and counts for `left`, `late_after` and `pass_newcomers`. A late joiner of a fixed group's loop therefore catches up only through barriers in `show_trial`: a group barrier inside the loop, such as one at the start of each iteration, waits for the newcomer's partners while they wait in the round for the newcomer, until `late_after` or `max_wait_time` moves someone on. PsyNet logs a warning naming both barriers when this happens; move the barrier into `show_trial`, or stop top-ups with `stop_top_ups=True` on a barrier before the module. In a module that a loop repeats, a member on a page between runs, outside the module, counts as having left it, so a faster member's next run starts without them and the group drifts out of step. PsyNet therefore raises a `ValueError` at load when a `while_loop` after a grouper shows content pages between runs of a module it repeats with no barrier between the pages and the next run; move the pages into the module, or add a `GroupBarrier` after them, outside the module. A barrier at the start of the module doesn't count, a loop's page makers (such as a `for_loop`) only warn, as their pages can't be seen at load, and the check can't tell whether the module is grouped and doesn't see a page in an outer module around the repeated one; the run-time warning gives the same advice and catches the cases the load-time check misses. One barrier ID used both inside `show_trial` and outside it acts as two separate barriers that can wait for each other in the same way; PsyNet logs a warning when members visit both, so give them distinct IDs.
