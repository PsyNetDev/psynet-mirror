The design-simulation guidance (`power-analysis` and `precision-estimation` skills) no longer sets a default precision threshold. The agent agrees the required precision with the experimenter, derives it from the research question if the experimenter has no view, uses power when the question is whether an effect exists, or presents precision and cost curves without a pass/fail decision. Margins of error are stated in the response's own units, such as rating points, and the report gives the smallest adequate design under each assumption set. For profiles, the guidance adds the margin of error of a difference, root mean square and largest margins across the set, the profile correlation and the smallest true spread at which the design reaches a 0.9 correlation, and it asks the analysis to report the achieved precision and split-half reliability after data collection. When an effect is stated on an observed scale, such as a correlation between a questionnaire score and behaviour, the simulation calibrates the response model to reproduce it. Design-simulation notebooks now lead with a plain-language summary and an example of what the results would look like, answer one question per figure with assumption sets named by what they mean, and keep definitions and the full metrics table in a final section for reviewers.
