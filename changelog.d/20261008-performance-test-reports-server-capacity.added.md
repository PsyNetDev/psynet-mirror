Each `psynet performance-test` run's summary now reports the server's capacity: the largest tested number of bots that kept the 95th-percentile response time of `/timeline` and `/response`, together and for each of them, under `--max-p95-ms` (default 500), kept the 95th-percentile async-process queue wait under `--max-queue-p95-s` (default 5), counting processes still queued when the test ends, had at most 0.1% of requests (one failed request is always allowed) and 1% of bots fail, and had at most 1% of requests answered as busy or stale (ten are always allowed). Busy (HTTP 503) and stale-page (HTTP 409) responses don't count as request errors; the report lists them on a separate "Busy/stale responses (409/503)" line. Once a larger count exceeded the limits, the summary names the limit it exceeded, or what made the bots fail (for example `HTTP 500 on /participant_status`), and suggests capping concurrent participants at 80% of the capacity; it also suggests adding worker processes when the async queue set the limit, and reports any errors in the test at the capacity it found. Tests whose bots didn't all start within the measurement window, or in which fewer than 90% of the requested bots were ever taking part at the same time, count as exceeding the limits. These, and bots failing because PostgreSQL ran out of connections, are limits of the test process rather than the server: when they are all that the smallest failing count exceeded, the summary reports the capacity as a lower bound, without a participant cap. If bots couldn't start fast enough, it explains that bots that take longer, in a longer experiment or with a larger `--time-factor`, give a better measure. Async processes left queued by one test are given time to start before the next.
